Foundations

UNIVERSAL FOUNDATIONS AND GENERAL RULES

5.1 PHYSICAL BLOOD IS REQUIRED

Every Blood Magic technique requires physical blood.

This is a defining foundation of the system: Blood Magic uses blood as its medium. A technique may use blood in different ways, such as a projectile, an infusion, a connection, a mark, or a reconstructed body, but physical blood is required for the technique to function.

The amount and source of blood required can vary by technique.

The requirement for physical blood does not mean that every technique must use the practitioner's own blood. Whether a technique can use the blood of another person, the practitioner's own blood, or either source is a separate matter. The default position is that other people's blood can be used, although unfamiliar blood is generally harder to control.

5.2 BLOOD AVAILABILITY IS A LIMITING FACTOR

The amount of blood a practitioner can access is a central practical limitation.

A technique's scale, duration, complexity, or demands may be constrained by the quantity of blood available. Different techniques can have different blood requirements. There is no universal numerical Blood Unit or fixed points pool.

Writers may introduce alternative sources, replenishment methods, or other ways of managing blood availability. They may also change or remove the ordinary limitations if that better suits the setting.

5.3 PHYSICAL STRAIN

Using too much of one's own blood can physically weaken or harm the practitioner. Excessive blood loss can have serious bodily consequences.

Overexerting one's ability to perform Blood Magic can also cause strain. The earlier design described this metaphorically as "spraining" one's blood: pushing beyond one's ability can cause pain, difficulty, or failure.

The exact symptoms, thresholds, recovery, and consequences are not universal. Writers may define them differently. This rule is a default limitation, not a required medical model.

5.4 ACTIVE CONSCIOUS CONTROL

The default rule is that blood must be actively and consciously controlled to move or perform an action through direct manipulation. When the practitioner stops controlling it, the blood does not continue performing that action on its own.

Writers may allow blood to retain instructions, act autonomously, or continue carrying out functions without active attention. Such behaviour is an optional variation, not the default.

This rule does not prevent a technique from leaving behind a persistent magical result. An object whose physical properties have been altered by Blood Armament can remain altered without the practitioner continuously controlling the blood inside it. A Covenant can remain binding after the formation technique ends. Such persistence belongs to the effect of the technique; it does not mean that the blood independently chooses new actions.

5.5 BLOOD CAN COME FROM DIFFERENT SOURCES

By default, practitioners can manipulate blood from themselves or other people. A practitioner's own blood is generally more familiar and easier to work with.

Other people's blood is usually less familiar. The comparison used in the design was like driving an unfamiliar car: the practitioner can operate it, but may not intuitively understand its particular behaviour as well as their own.

This unfamiliarity is not necessarily active resistance by the blood's owner. It is a control and familiarity issue.

Writers may change or remove the distinction between familiar and unfamiliar blood.

5.6 BLOOD CAN ENTER AND REMAIN INSIDE MATERIALS

The default system allows blood to enter materials and remain inside them. It can exert physical forces from within a material or object.

This capability is especially important to Blood Armament, but is not limited to the discipline's defining identity. General Blood Manipulation can also interact with blood that has entered a material.

The default model treats the blood as a substance that can occupy the inside of another material. The precise physical and chemical relationship between blood and the material is writer-defined.

5.7 PENETRATING MATERIALS

The developed default concept is that Blood Magic can force blood into materials, moving it through fibres, gaps, pores, or other available structures. Density and material structure affect how difficult this is.

In the earlier design, magic could force absorption even where ordinary liquid would not naturally enter. More resistant or dense materials could be harder to penetrate.

Writers can change whether all materials can be penetrated, whether some materials are immune or resistant, and how material structure changes the difficulty.

5.8 DISTRIBUTION AND CONCENTRATION

Blood can be evenly distributed or concentrated in particular areas. A practitioner is not necessarily restricted to keeping all controlled blood in one uniform mass.

Blood can also enter a material or target from multiple points of contact, where a technique permits it.

The degree of precision, number of independently controlled portions, and difficulty of complex distributions are writer-defined.

5.9 THE PHYSICAL AND CHEMICAL NATURE OF BLOOD

This is deliberately writer-defined.

A writer decides whether blood remains physically and chemically ordinary blood, whether magic changes its physical or chemical properties, whether it can transform into other substances, and whether it possesses additional supernatural properties beyond being manipulable.

The system does not require every setting to use the same answer.

5.10 BLOOD AGE AND CONDITION

The recommended default is that age alone does not automatically make blood unusable. Its physical condition may matter more than its age.

Fresh, dried, contaminated, preserved, or otherwise altered blood may behave differently depending on the world's rules.

Whether old blood retains magical usefulness, whether blood naturally deteriorates for magical purposes, and how preservation works are writer-defined.

5.11 COMBINING BLOOD FROM DIFFERENT PEOPLE

The recommended default is that blood from different people can be combined. There is no universal incompatibility that makes combination impossible.

Controlling a mixture may be more difficult because it contains blood from multiple sources and may involve unfamiliar patterns of control.

Writers decide whether the sources remain distinguishable, whether mixing changes the blood's properties, and how difficult combined control becomes.

5.12 RESISTANCE AND COUNTERING

As a default, another practitioner may be able to resist or interfere with attempts to manipulate their blood. The degree and nature of that resistance are not fully fixed.

The design distinguishes between unfamiliarity and resistance:

  • - Unfamiliarity means a practitioner has less intuitive control over another person's blood.
  • - Resistance means the target or another practitioner actively opposes the manipulation, where the setting allows this.

Writers decide whether non-practitioners can resist, how powerful resistance can be, and whether particular techniques bypass or overcome it.

5.13 BLOOD AND ITS SOURCE

The relationship between blood and the person or being it came from is entirely writer-defined.

Blood may or may not retain a supernatural connection to its source. Possible forms of connection include biological information, memories, emotional traces, personality, behavioural tendencies, or a link to the original owner. None of these possibilities is compulsory.

5.14 SPONTANEOUS USE AND PREPARATION

Blood Magic can be used spontaneously. Mandatory preparation is not a universal requirement.

Some techniques may require preparation, marks, infusions, agreements, or other prior steps. These requirements belong to the particular technique rather than to Blood Magic as a whole.

5.15 MATERIAL LIMITATIONS AND PHYSICAL REALITY

Blood Magic does not automatically imply that matter can be created from nothing. The earlier design treated the use of existing materials as the basis for crafting and reshaping objects.

Whether a writer permits material creation, transmutation, or other exceptions is a worldbuilding decision. Blood Armament's established identity is the alteration of physical properties in existing crafted objects, not the automatic creation of matter.

5.16 NO UNIVERSAL NUMERICAL RESOURCE POOL

Blood availability is measured conceptually through the blood actually available and the demands of the technique. No standardised Blood Unit, mana pool, spell slot, or other numerical reserve is required.

5.17 FORBIDDEN TECHNIQUES

Manipulating blood inside living people is generally forbidden in the default social and legal framing. The prohibition is comparable in seriousness to a war crime in the setting that uses this assumption.

This is a societal or legal principle, not an intrinsic magical barrier. Writers can change, weaken, or remove the prohibition. A setting might also have supernatural systems that enforce legal restrictions, such as a mandatory Blood Covenant.
