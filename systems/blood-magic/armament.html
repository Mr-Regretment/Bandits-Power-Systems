The Five Disciplines

BLOOD ARMAMENT

8.1 CORE IDENTITY

Blood Armament is the discipline of infusing materials with blood to give crafted objects personalised physical properties and a connection to their creator.

It is an extension of conventional craftsmanship, not simply a method of conjuring magical weapons.

Blood Armament is the only formal disciple whose defining speciality is changing the physical properties of objects and materials.

8.2 DEFINING PROPERTY

Blood Armament always allows blood to change an object's physical properties.

This is the defining feature of the discipline. The particular properties changed, how they change, and any additional capabilities are writer-defined.

8.3 INFUSION AND THE MATERIAL

In the established default concept, blood enters a material and remains inside it. The object remains the object it originally was. A steel sword infused with blood is still a steel sword containing blood; it is not automatically a separate entity or a generic "blood construct."

The blood can be manipulated inside the object. It can exert force from within and can alter the object's physical behaviour according to the rules chosen by the writer.

The exact physical or chemical relationship between the blood and the material is writer-defined under the general rules of Blood Magic.

8.4 PERSISTENCE

A central established capability is that infused blood can remain in an object without the practitioner continuously concentrating on it.

The resulting altered properties can persist as an effect of Blood Armament. This does not mean the blood must autonomously perform new actions. Any active movement or adjustment of blood inside the object can still require conscious control under the default rule.

8.5 CONNECTION TO THE CREATOR

Blood Armament gives the infused object a connection to its creator. The exact nature and strength of that connection are writer-defined.

Possible applications include a sense of the object's location or condition, an advantage in controlling its infused blood, or the ability to recall it. None of these examples is compulsory in every version.

8.6 WRITER-DEFINED PROPERTIES AND APPLICATIONS

Writers decide:

  • - Which physical properties are altered.
  • - How those properties are altered.
  • - Whether the object gains additional capabilities.
  • - How much blood is needed for different objects.
  • - Whether blood quantity affects flexibility, hardness, or other properties.
  • - Whether the object can become flexible, move, repair itself, reshape itself, or be recalled.
  • - Whether the object can sense or communicate its location or condition to its creator.
  • - Whether multiple people can contribute blood to one object.
  • - Whether another practitioner can take control of the infused blood.
  • - Whether infused blood can be replaced with blood from another source.
  • - Whether blood can bridge damage or gaps in an object.
  • - Whether fragments can be reconstructed.
  • - How many objects can be infused or controlled at once.
  • - How attention is divided between several objects.
  • - Which materials are compatible and how easily they accept blood.
  • - How object size, intricacy, complexity, and material affect the process.
  • - How much force an infused object can exert.
  • - How many special properties one object can possess.
  • - The range and conditions of any recall ability.
  • - Whether recall requires sight and whether weight or anchors matter.

8.7 EARLIER MATERIAL-PROPERTY MODEL (OPTIONAL EXAMPLE)

An earlier detailed implementation explored the following model. These are examples, not universal requirements:

  • - Blood penetrated materials through their fibres, pores, gaps, or other structures; density and construction affected the difficulty.
  • - Blood remained inside the object as a separate liquid substance.
  • - More blood could make an object softer or more flexible, while less blood could make it harder or stiffer.
  • - A flexible sword could be stiffened on command.
  • - Blood itself did not become rigid; the material supplied the object's hardness.
  • - Internal blood channels could be created by skilled practitioners.
  • - Blood could move through those channels when consciously controlled.
  • - Blood could exert internal force, allowing an object to flex, twist, move, or reshape.
  • - A healing-channel arrangement could allow an object to repair damage. More blood could improve repair while reducing hardness.
  • - Reshaping had no established absolute limit in theory, but material, size, and intricacy made a difference in practice.
  • - An object could be reshaped back to an earlier form.
  • - Multiple materials, such as wood, metal, and string, could be combined; ease depended on the materials' structure and ability to accept blood.
  • - Multiple practitioners could contribute blood to the same object.
  • - Another practitioner could potentially drain or take over infused blood, while the current user could counter that attempt.
  • - If the blood escaped from an object, the properties it provided could be lost. The blood might be siphoned back into the practitioner.
  • - Replacing an object's infused blood with blood from another source could be difficult, like reverse-engineering an unfamiliar system.
  • - A damaged object could be bent back into shape. Fluid blood might bridge a missing-material gap, although that area would be weaker.
  • - Fragments could preserve enough of an object's outline or geometry to allow progressive reconstruction.
  • - Living materials such as wood, leather, or bone could be infused.
  • - Controlling multiple objects was possible, but divided attention could make it difficult.

8.8 EARLIER RECALL EXAMPLE (OPTIONAL)

One earlier version explored a recall ability reminiscent of a weapon returning to its owner. In that example:

  • - Recall from 0 to 10 feet could work without sight.
  • - Recall from 10 to 20 feet required sight.
  • - Recall beyond 20 feet was unavailable.
  • - Heavier objects were more difficult to recall.
  • - Anchors or opposing forces could prevent or challenge recall.
  • - The creator had a faint intuitive sense of the object's location or condition.

These distances and restrictions are not universal. Recall itself is an optional application of the creator connection.

8.9 FAMILIARITY AND CONTROL BY OTHERS

An earlier model gave the creator an advantage because they were familiar with the arrangement of their own infused blood. Other practitioners could potentially manipulate the blood, but might initially control it poorly until they understood its arrangement.

This is consistent with the general idea that unfamiliar blood or an unfamiliar arrangement is harder to control. Whether this applies in a particular version is writer-defined.
