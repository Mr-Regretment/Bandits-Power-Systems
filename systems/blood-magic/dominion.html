The Five Disciplines

BLOOD DOMINION

9.1 CORE IDENTITY

Blood Dominion is a discipline that connects a practitioner's movements to selected muscle groups in another person. Once activated, the selected muscle groups mirror the practitioner's corresponding movements.

The original inspiration was a movement-mirroring idea. The blood mark is a physical mechanism for establishing the link; it is not an ordinary shadow and the technique does not manipulate a person's literal shadow.

The detailed mechanics below are the version retained for the current design. They have deliberately been left unchanged for now.

9.2 SELECTION OF MUSCLE GROUPS

Before activation, the practitioner selects the specific muscle groups they want to affect.

The practitioner touches the target and places physical blood marks on the selected muscle groups. Several marks can be placed before the technique is activated.

The practitioner chooses the exact moment of activation. Activation locks in the selection. Marks placed after activation do not add new muscle groups to the technique.

9.3 BLOOD CONNECTIONS

Each mark is linked to the practitioner by a physical stream of blood. The streams connect the practitioner to the target's marked muscle groups.

The streams can bend and stretch around obstacles, but only up to a limit. The exact maximum distance or degree of flexibility has not been specified.

The technique affects one target at a time.

9.4 MIRRORING

Once activated, each selected muscle group perfectly mirrors the practitioner's corresponding movements.

The practitioner must physically move the corresponding body parts for the target's muscles to mirror them. The practitioner does not simply issue an abstract instruction for the target to perform an unrelated action.

The mirroring has no built-in adjustment for speed or intensity. The selected muscles copy the corresponding movements as defined by the technique.

The target's nervous system is bypassed. The target does not need to voluntarily perform the movement for the marked muscles to move. The technique can therefore move muscles even where the target cannot voluntarily move them due to paralysis or another impairment affecting voluntary movement. The target's ordinary physical limitations still apply.

9.5 RESISTANCE

The target can oppose the imposed movement. Resistance is comparable to trying to move through thick water: the target can make the movement harder, but cannot simply break the technique completely through that opposition.

Resistance increases blood consumption.

9.6 FORCE AND BLOOD FLOW

The controlling force is determined by the blood flow or amount supplied to the target, not by the practitioner's physical strength.

The flow rate is automatically determined by the technique. The practitioner cannot manually adjust it at will.

9.7 SHARED SUPPLY

The blood supply is shared among all active marks.

All active connections are supplied until the available supply becomes insufficient. There is no deliberate priority system in which the practitioner chooses which mark receives blood first.

Higher total demand results in higher total consumption. Movement, small adjustments, and resistance can change the demand dynamically. Stillness lowers demand, although it may not eliminate it completely.

If the practitioner has insufficient blood to keep every connection active, connections fail individually as their needs can no longer be met. The remaining connections can continue operating.

9.8 INTERRUPTED CONNECTIONS

If a physical blood stream is interrupted, supply to the corresponding mark stops. However, blood already in the stream continues travelling for a few seconds.

When that remaining blood reaches the mark, the corresponding muscle group stops mirroring.

The same grace period applies if the practitioner runs out of blood: residual blood already travelling through a connection can still reach its mark before the movement stops.

Interrupting one stream affects only the corresponding muscle group. It does not automatically deactivate the other connections.

9.9 FAILED MARKS AND REACTIVATION

Once a connection fails, the old mark cannot be reactivated. A new mark would be required.

However, marks placed after activation do not affect the active selection. The practitioner must therefore establish any required new marks before activating a new instance of the technique.

When a connection fails, or when the practitioner ends the technique, the mark remains as ordinary blood. It no longer functions as an active Dominion mark.

9.10 SUMMARY OF THE RETAINED MECHANICS

  • - One target at a time.
  • - The practitioner selects muscle groups before activation.
  • - Blood marks are physically placed on those groups.
  • - Activation locks in the selected groups.
  • - Each mark has a physical blood stream connecting it to the practitioner.
  • - Streams can bend or stretch around obstacles up to an unspecified limit.
  • - Selected muscles mirror the practitioner's corresponding movements.
  • - The practitioner must make the corresponding movements physically.
  • - The target's nervous system is bypassed.
  • - Physical limitations still apply.
  • - The target can resist movement, increasing blood consumption, but cannot simply break the technique through resistance.
  • - Blood demand is dynamic and automatically distributed.
  • - Each connection can fail individually if its supply is insufficient or interrupted.
  • - Residual blood in a stream gives a short grace period after interruption or depletion.
  • - Failed marks cannot be reused within the same activation.
  • - Marks become ordinary blood when the technique ends.
