The Five Disciplines

BLOOD COVENANT

10.1 CORE IDENTITY

Blood Covenant is the discipline of creating supernatural agreements between participants. It makes agreed terms binding through Blood Magic, allowing violations to be detected and punished without requiring a human judge or continual human supervision.

The discipline originated conceptually from people who attempted to stop a great war through contracts and agreements.

The following mechanics are the completed version established in the design. They should be treated as retained rules unless a writer intentionally changes the system.

10.2 FORMING A COVENANT

To form a Covenant:

  • 1. The participants hold hands in a circle.
  • 2. All participants agree to the terms.
  • 3. Each participant contributes exactly one drop of their own blood.
  • 4. The participants activate the technique.
  • 5. Magical marks appear on the participants' palms.

Each person contributes their own drop. The contributions are not pooled as a single shared payment before the Covenant is formed.

10.3 COVENANT MARKS

Each participant receives a magical palm mark.

The marks:

  • - Can be hidden.
  • - Cannot be damaged or removed.
  • - Are visually identifiable as Covenant marks.
  • - Do not reveal the Covenant's rules to an ordinary observer merely because the observer can see the mark.

A marked participant instinctively knows the rules of the Covenant.

Someone using basic Blood Manipulation can reveal the rules by actively attempting to learn them.

Tampering with a Covenant mark causes excruciating pain to the person interfering with it. This pain is the established global punishment for attempted mark tampering.

10.4 TERMS AND SPECIFICITY

Covenant terms must be extremely specific. They should be drafted with the care of a legal contract.

The technique detects whether the terms have been violated. It does not require a person to monitor every participant or decide whether a violation occurred.

Because the Covenant enforces the terms as written, imprecise or ambiguous wording can create problems. The exact consequences of ambiguous wording have not been separately defined; a writer may decide how their version handles it.

10.5 DETECTING AND PUNISHING VIOLATIONS

The Covenant magically detects violations of its terms. Accidental violations count as violations too, unless the terms themselves make an exception.

Each violation can have an assigned punishment. Pain is the default punishment, but a Covenant can specify a different punishment.

There are no supernatural limits on the kinds of punishment that can be specified beyond the laws of the setting's world and what is physically possible under those laws.

Two named punishment concepts were established:

  • - PAIN: The default punishment for a violation or for tampering with the mark.
  • - BLOOD DIMINISHMENT: A punishment in which the offender loses blood for violating the Covenant.

The precise implementation of punishments is determined by the terms and by the setting's rules.

10.6 MEMBERSHIP AND JOINING

There is no fixed maximum number of participants. A Covenant can theoretically include an unlimited number of people.

A new participant can join an existing Covenant by agreeing to its terms and contributing one drop of their own blood. They receive a Covenant mark and become bound by its rules.

A participant may belong to multiple Covenants, provided the terms do not conflict in a way that makes simultaneous compliance impossible.

10.7 LEAVING, RELEASE, AND DEATH

A participant cannot simply choose to leave a Covenant.

A participant remains bound until the Covenant's duration or completion condition is met, or until they are released by a majority of the other participants.

The person whose release is being considered does not vote on their own release.

When released:

  • - Their obligations end immediately.
  • - Their associated punishments no longer apply.
  • - Pending punishments are also removed.
  • - Their original blood contribution remains part of the Covenant's history or structure; it is not returned merely because they are released.

Death is treated exactly like release. A deceased participant is removed from the Covenant in the same way as a released participant.

Covenants may be indefinite. They do not require a fixed end date if the terms do not specify one.

10.8 VOTING

Covenant decisions use one vote per participant.

A majority is required. Abstentions are excluded from the count.

A participant who abstains does not contribute a yes or no vote to the majority calculation.

10.9 THE BLOOD COIN

If a vote ends in a 50-50 tie, a literal Blood Coin forms from blood associated with that Covenant.

The coin has two sides representing the two possible outcomes. It is flipped to decide the tied vote.

The coin only forms when a tie occurs. After the result can be read, the coin vanishes.

The result of the coin flip is final and cannot be challenged for that vote. However, the participants can hold a new vote later and change the decision through the ordinary voting process.

10.10 AMENDING TERMS

A majority can amend the Covenant's rules.

Changes take effect immediately. Participants can therefore become bound by terms that they did not personally vote for, provided the amendment passed under the Covenant's voting rules.

A change can also retroactively alter the punishment assigned to an already committed violation, as long as the punishment has not yet been carried out.

10.11 DISSOLUTION

A Covenant cannot simply be dissolved by a vote while two or more participants remain.

It disappears when fewer than two participants remain.

This means a Covenant can persist indefinitely while it retains at least two participants, subject to its terms, releases, deaths, and other established rules.

10.12 MULTIPLE COVENANTS

An individual can join multiple Covenants. The practical limitation is that the terms must not conflict in a way that prevents compliance.

The discipline does not impose a separate universal maximum number of Covenants per participant.

10.13 COVENANTS AS SOCIAL OR LEGAL INFRASTRUCTURE

A world may use Blood Covenant as part of its legal or social system. For example, a society could teach Covenant to children and require citizens to enter a Universal Blood Covenant by law. The terms might restrict forbidden techniques or establish common obligations.

This is an optional worldbuilding application, not a universal feature of every Blood Magic setting.
