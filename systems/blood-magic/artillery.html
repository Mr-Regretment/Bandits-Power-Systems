The Five Disciplines

BLOOD ARTILLERY

7.1 CORE IDENTITY

Blood Artillery is the discipline of launching blood as a projectile and directing it after launch.

Its defining ideas are:

  • - Blood is launched as a projectile.
  • - The practitioner can direct the projectile.
  • - The projectile can interact with mana-based magic in compatible versions of the system.

This identity is more important than any one projectile size, speed, range, or collision rule.

7.2 THE PROJECTILE

The basic concept is that a practitioner expels or otherwise uses blood, shapes it into a projectile, and launches it.

The projectile remains blood-based. Its shape, size, speed, range, number, persistence, and exact physical behaviour are writer-defined.

Other people's blood can be used in the broader system by default, but how easily unfamiliar blood can be shaped into an Artillery projectile is writer-defined.

7.3 DIRECTION AND CONTROL

The practitioner can direct the projectile. The extent of steering, precision, required concentration, and whether control can be shared or interrupted are writer-defined.

7.4 COLLISIONS AND DISPERSAL

The way a projectile behaves when it hits an obstacle or target is writer-defined. A version may have the blood bend around an obstacle, smash through it, disperse, split, or behave in another way.

A version may allow the practitioner to choose which collision counts as the projectile's final collision. What happens at that collision is also writer-defined.

7.5 INTERACTION WITH MANA-BASED MAGIC

Blood Artillery can interact with mana-based magic. An established example is placing a mana spell inside a blood projectile so that the projectile carries the spell while travelling.

The exact interface between the two systems remains writer-defined. The Artillery discipline does not inherently make Blood Magic dependent on mana.

7.6 EARLIER PROJECTILE IMPLEMENTATION (OPTIONAL EXAMPLE)

The following mechanics appeared in an earlier detailed version. They are preserved as an example of one possible implementation, not as mandatory rules:

  • - The practitioner expelled their own blood and shaped it into a projectile.
  • - The projectile could take any shape within a bounding box no larger than 0.75 metres on each side.
  • - The blood remained liquid and generally tended to come back together after separating, subject to the particular collision or final-impact rules of that implementation.
  • - When encountering a wall or obstacle, it could either smash through or bend around it.
  • - The practitioner could freely steer it during flight.
  • - The practitioner selected which collision counted as the final collision.
  • - At the final collision, the blood splashed apart and became ordinary, uncontrolled blood.
  • - A larger projectile required more blood.
  • - The original version included a mana-spell payload, with no more than one mana spell inside a projectile.
  • - The caster could activate the contained spell remotely at any point during flight, or arrange for it to activate automatically on the selected final collision if it had not already been activated.

The exact projectile size, the one-spell limit, the use of the practitioner's own blood, speed, range, simultaneous projectile count, concentration, persistence, splitting, collision rules, and activation conditions are all writer-defined in the current broad design.

7.7 MANA SPELL PAYLOAD: INTERFACE EXAMPLE

The separate mana system previously discussed has a mechanism in which a practitioner contains a mana spell and embeds it in a compatible mystical object. The first activation by a user while using or being at the object consumes the stored activation without requiring the user's mana. That stored activation is used once; subsequent uses require the ordinary mana cost, cast at the object.

Blood Artillery's possible use of this mechanic is an example of the two systems interacting. The exact compatibility rules, containment method, activation conditions, and number of spells per projectile remain writer-defined.
